# FACTS — Inner Factory series (W48)

Every number spoken or shown, with its source. Metaphor simplifications are listed under each episode.

## E1 — The Mucus Escalator
- ~11,000 L of air breathed per day — How It Works, "5 amazing facts about human respiration": "On average, one individual will breathe in 11,000 litres of air a day." https://www.howitworksdaily.com/top-5-facts-respiration/
- ~200 cilia per cell; beat 10–20 times per second; mucus moved to the pharynx and swallowed or coughed up — Wikipedia, "Mucociliary clearance": "around 200 cilia", "between 10 and 20 times per second", moved to the pharynx "either swallowed or coughed up". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucociliary_clearance
- Stomach acid breaks it down: swallowed mucus enters the stomach (acid digestion) — general physiology; the voice says "finishes the job", not "sterilises everything".
- Simplification: goblet cells + glands drawn as one "glue press"; trachea drawn as one escalator.

## E2 — 120 Days
- Lifespan ~120 days; made in bone marrow at ~2 million per second; removed in spleen, liver and bone marrow — Stanford Blood Center: "They are produced in the bone marrow of large bones at a rate of 2 million per second"; "removed from the blood circulation in the spleen, liver and bone marrow". https://stanfordbloodcenter.org/how_long_do_red/
- Ageing membranes → recognised by macrophages; iron carried by transferrin back to the bone marrow and reused — LibreTexts A&P, "RBC Life Cycle". https://med.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Anatomy_and_Physiology/Anatomy_and_Physiology_(Boundless)/16%3A_Cardiovascular_System_-_Blood/16.2%3A_Red_Blood_Cells/16.2C%3A_RBC_Life_Cycle
- Simplification: the spleen "inspection slit" stands for splenic filtering of less deformable cells; the liver and marrow also remove old cells (not shown). "Your iron goes back to the marrow": some of the iron is reused (not all).

## E3 — Why Knuckles Crack
- Sound = gas cavity/bubble forming in synovial fluid (cavitation), shown in a 2015 real-time MRI study (University of Alberta, Kawchuk et al., PLOS ONE); ~20 min before the same joint can crack again; Dr. Donald Unger cracked one hand's knuckles for more than 60 years, no arthritis in either hand (Arthritis & Rheumatism letter, 1998; Ig Nobel 2009) — Acibadem International summary. https://acibademinternational.com/blog/is-it-bad-to-crack-your-knuckles-the-60-year-experiment-that-answered-it/
- "If it hurts, see a doctor" — general safety line.
- Simplification: one finger joint drawn as a sealed tank with an oil bath; one doctor = one person, not a study (the voice says "one doctor").

## E4 — The Sweat of Humanity
- ~2 million functional eccrine glands (anatomical estimates 2–4 million); daily insensible water loss 0.6–2.3 L for a 1.8 m² person — Taylor & Machado-Moreira, Extreme Physiology & Medicine 2013 (PMC3710196). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3710196
- Calculation (conservative 0.5 L/day, below the 0.6 L lower bound): 0.5 × 365 = 182.5 L/person/year ("about 180"); × 8.2 billion people (UN World Population Prospects 2024, 2025 estimate) = 1.50 trillion L/year; Olympic pool ≈ 2,500,000 L (50 × 25 × 2 m) → 1.5e12 / 2.5e6 = 600,000 pools.
- Wording: "at least half a liter of water a day" through the skin (sweat + vapour), not "half a liter of sweat".

## E5 — Your Bones Sound the Alarm
- Berger J.M. et al., "Mediation of the Acute Stress Response by the Skeleton", Cell Metabolism 2019: stressors in mice, rats and humans cause a "rapid and selective surge of circulating bioactive osteocalcin"; osteocalcin inhibits post-synaptic parasympathetic neurons (so sympathetic tone is unopposed = "takes the brakes off"); "adrenalectomized rodents and patients with adrenal insufficiency can still mount an ASR". https://www.dpag.ox.ac.uk/publications/1054518
- Wording: "might be the reason" (findings mostly in rodents); "bone cells" = osteoblasts.
- Simplification: amygdala drawn as a control room; adrenal glands as a pump station that is switched off.
