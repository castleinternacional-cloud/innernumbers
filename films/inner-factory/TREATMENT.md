# TREATMENT — "Inner Factory" (Inner Numbers, week 48)

Five vertical Shorts (1080×1920, 24 fps, ~30 s each) in the Assembly-Line Diagram style. One series look, five different factories. English only: plaques read `MACHINE | REAL NAME`, both in English. Narration: ElevenLabs "Ryan – American Documentary Narrator". Each script is our own, written after studying the structure of a short that over-performed in Spanish or Portuguese (research/shorts-w48-estructuras.md); every number is in FACTS.md.

## Series structure — three candidates

1. **One giant body, five tours.** The same cut-away person in every episode; each Short rides a different cargo through a different floor. Pro: instant series recognition. Con: every opening looks the same; the hand (E3) and the seated sweat (E4) don't fit one standing body.
2. **A tour, then a number.** Each Short ends on one huge counter (11,000 L, 120 days, 20 min, 1.5 trillion L…). Pro: "counted" is the brand. Con: alone it's a list, not a story.
3. **Five factories, one rule: the outside world triggers the line, a number pays it off, the last frame loops into the first.** Each Short builds the silhouette its topic needs (a breathing torso, a vein network, a hand, a seated person, a running body); opens with the outside world through a porthole or on a belt; the turn is a native move (gatekeeper, follow the colour, warehouse clock, porthole alarm); the payoff is one counter; the last frame matches the first (Shorts loop).

**Chosen: 3**, with 2's counter as the payoff of every episode. It keeps the brand ("your body, counted"), it obeys the source structures (weird image → belief broken → mechanism → twist), and it avoids five identical openings.

## Benchmark

- Fritz Kahn's body-as-factory plates (grammar: rooms, numbered stations, pipes as legend) and Nigel Holmes's TIME explainers (thick outline, flat colour, the joke said straight).
- Learn: one direction of travel; one colour = one meaning; workers as scale; the number lands on a downbeat.
- Don't take: any plate's layout, period lettering, aged paper, or the library demo's sandwich/factory opening and running-figure ending.

## Look (all episodes)

Palette "Night Shift, Inner Numbers": paper #F2ECE0, ink #1D2030, ground #24345A (inside the cut), wall #FBF7EE, body #EE9F87; spot colours with ONE meaning across the series: vermilion #E04A2F = blood / alarm, ochre #F3B23A = numbers, energy and iron, teal #2E8D87 = air and water, plus olive #9AA43B for mucus (E1 only). Station circles ochre. Fonts: Big Shoulders Display 800–900 (plaques, tags, counters), Archivo 600–800 (tickets, labels). Subtitles are work tickets bottom-left, ink frame, stub with the current station number (a clock when none); 2 lines max, Archivo 44 px (vertical frame). Brand nameplate "INNER NUMBERS" bolted to the first station of every episode (an object, not a header).

## Score & sound (all episodes)

March at 120 BPM (beat = 0.5 s, bar = 2 s), one voice per station: pizzicato bass + woodblock base; each station adds a voice (bassoon, clarinet, flute, glockenspiel on the counter). The gatekeeper / alarm stops the band dead (true silence ≥ 0.5 s), restarts on release. Foley: procedural (sfx.py) for presses, rollers, chains, sign bounces, clock ticks, pops, bells. Voice compressed, ~10 dB over music, music carved 300 Hz–4 kHz under it. −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dB.

## E1 — The Mucus Escalator (source structure: La Hiperactina, 17.3×)

Logline: the dirt you breathe is glued, carried up a hair escalator and swallowed without you noticing.
Arc: weird image (a blob of lung mucus on the belt, plaque "SNOT? | LUNG MUCUS") → belief broken ("not from your nose") → purpose (flypaper) → question → escalator → twist (you swallow it) → loop.
Silhouette: a torso in profile with head; airway as a tall vertical shaft (fits 9:16).
Shots:
1. 0–3 s · Insert, static → slow push: a green blob on a belt under a plaque. Why: the strange image is the hook.
2. 3–8 s · Porthole POV (circular mask) at the nose intake: dust, pollen, a germ sail in; counter "11,000 L / DAY" flips on the intake gauge. Why: outside world triggers the line.
3. 8–13 s · Tilt down the shaft into ① GLUE PRESS | GOBLET CELLS: the press drips sticky sheets; a dust particle lands and sticks. Station start.
4. 13–21 s · Signature: the camera rides the mucus sheet UP ② HAIR ESCALATOR | CILIA — rows of tiny oars beating on the beat, counter "200 HAIRS / CELL · 20 BEATS / SEC". Tilt up (the reverse of the usual chute).
5. 21–27 s · ③ JUNCTION | THROAT: a worker with a lever tips the load over into the down-chute; whip down to ④ ACID DRUM | STOMACH; the drum spins, gauge hits DONE.
6. 27–30 s · Pull back to the whole torso; a worker taps a gauge "RUNNING NOW"; last frame = first frame's belt (loop).
Native move: tilt up the escalator riding the cargo. Cue: the swallow lands on a downbeat after one bar of silence ("And then?").

## E2 — 120 Days (source: La Hiperactina, 7.6×)

Logline: you are a red blood cell; on day 120 the spleen fails you, takes you apart, and sends your iron home to build the next you.
Arc: second person + number in the hook → birth → routine → turn (stiff) → inspection (gatekeeper) → recycling → rebirth (loop).
Silhouette: a standing figure, cut away at the hip (marrow in the pelvis/femur) and the left abdomen (spleen); veins as red pipes.
Shots:
1. 0–4 s · Close: a red disc pops out of a stamping press "DAY 1" ticket clipped on; ① STAMPING PLANT | BONE MARROW, counter "2,000,000 / SEC" on the press. Hook = number + "you".
2. 4–10 s · Truck with the cell along the red pipe; two docks: teal (O₂ drop-off) and grey (CO₂ pick-up), workers swap crates, repeated three times faster (time compression). Day clock spins to 119.
3. 10–14 s · Insert: the cell's rim gets a "STIFF" stencil; it wobbles slower.
4. 14–21 s · ② INSPECTION SLIT | SPLEEN: gatekeeper native move — bendy cells squeeze through a narrow slit; ours stops; worker raises STOP; music stops dead.
5. 21–26 s · ③ SCRAP YARD | MACROPHAGES: a claw takes it apart into colours: ochre iron beads into the ochre pipe, the rest to a bin.
6. 26–30 s · Follow the colour: whip along the ochre pipe back to the marrow press, which stamps a fresh cell "DAY 1" → loop.

## E3 — Why Knuckles Crack (source: La Hiperactina, 3.0×)

Logline: a crack is a bubble being born in a sealed oil tank — and the man who cracked one hand for 60 years got arthritis in neither.
Silhouette: a giant hand (vertical: fingers up), one knuckle cut open as a pressurised tank.
Shots:
1. 0–3 s · Wide: the hand silhouette, a gantry crane hooked to the index finger. Question on a ticket.
2. 3–9 s · Push through the knuckle door into ① OIL TANK | SYNOVIAL JOINT: sealed capsule, workers floating on oil.
3. 9–14 s · The crane pulls; pressure gauge needle drops; dissolved gas beads gather → one bubble swells → POP (the loudest sound of the film after a beat of silence).
4. 14–18 s · ② RESET CLOCK: warehouse-and-clock native move — the clock spins 20 minutes, workers doze, gas dissolves back.
5. 18–26 s · Porthole: a doctor's two hands on a desk; a tally board for the LEFT hand fills (60 years, ~36,500 cracks); the right hand board empty. Both hands get an "ARTHRITIS: NONE" stamp.
6. 26–30 s · A worker hangs a small sign "IF IT HURTS → DOCTOR"; pull back to the hand; the crane hooks the finger again (loop).

## E4 — The Sweat of Humanity (source: Ciência Todo Dia, 2.1 M views)

Logline: your skin quietly leaks half a liter a day; times eight billion people, that's 600,000 Olympic pools a year.
Silhouette: a person sitting on a chair (sitting still is the point), the skin wall full of tiny pumps.
Shots:
1. 0–3 s · Close: one drop leaves a pore pump; a measuring cup under it. Question.
2. 3–8 s · Pull back: ① PUMP WALL | SWEAT GLANDS — rows of tiny pumps along the body wall, counter "2,000,000".
3. 8–13 s · The collector pipe (teal) fills a jug "0.5 L / DAY" under the chair, worker in an armchair (sitting still).
4. 13–18 s · Warehouse-and-clock: the calendar flips a year; jugs pile into a barrel "180 L / YEAR".
5. 18–25 s · The great pull-back: one barrel becomes a field of tiny seated people, the counter rolls "8,200,000,000 × 180 L"; teal pipes join into one river.
6. 25–30 s · The river fills a grid of Olympic pools; counter "600,000 POOLS"; cut back to the single drop (loop).

## E5 — Your Bones Sound the Alarm (source: CuriosaMente, 528 K)

Logline: the old story said adrenal pumps start the panic; in 2019 bone turned out to be pulling the brake off.
Silhouette: a standing person; control room in the head, adrenal pump station at the waist, a bone factory inside the thigh, a heart pump in the chest.
Shots:
1. 0–4 s · Porthole alarm: through the eye window a car swerves at us; control room ① ALARM DESK | AMYGDALA: red lamp, worker slaps the button.
2. 4–10 s · "The old story": follow the red wire down to ② PUMP STATION | ADRENAL GLANDS, labelled on an old dusty plaque; pump starts.
3. 10–15 s · Turn: the pump station's lights go off (rodents without adrenals) — yet the alarm keeps ringing; a question-mark-shaped empty hook swings.
4. 15–22 s · Tilt down to ③ BONE FACTORY | OSTEOBLASTS in the thigh: presses stamp ochre capsules "OSTEOCALCIN" into the red pipe. Signature: follow the ochre colour up to ④ BRAKE LEVER | PARASYMPATHETIC; a capsule knocks the brake off.
5. 22–27 s · Heart pump races, lung bellows speed, leg pistons start — the body runs (pistons, not a walking rig).
6. 27–30 s · Pull back: the skeleton glows ochre inside the silhouette, siren lamp on top; back to the eye porthole (loop).

## Beat sheet & cue map

Timings are placed from the ElevenLabs voice word timestamps (faster-whisper), then snapped to the 0.5 s grid: every station start, counter lock and STOP lands on a beat; each voice line starts on a beat; the counter and the swallow/pop/STOP land on a bar line. One bar of silence before each turn ("And then?", "Old ones?", "Pop.", "One and a half trillion", "The missing piece?").

## Sound design table

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | factory room tone (filtered noise) | belt rattle, the hook object's sound | pizz bass + woodblock, 1 bar |
| Stations | room tone | each machine on its cycle (press, rollers, pumps, claw) | one voice added per station |
| Turn | room tone only | — (true silence ≥ 0.5 s) | band stops dead |
| Payoff | room tone | counter ticks, bell on lock | full band + glockenspiel |
| Loop out | — | the hook's sound again | first bar again (loops) |

## Subtitles & titles

Work tickets bottom-left (stub = station number), ≤ 2 lines, ≤ 7 words a line, held ≥ max(1.8 s, line + 0.6 s). Counters are objects (a gauge face, a flip board on a machine), never floating text. The episode title is the plaque of the first station (e.g. "THE MUCUS ESCALATOR" on the intake gantry).
